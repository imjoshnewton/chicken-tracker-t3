## Keeping it Simple

If you’re considering adding chickens to your backyard, you may be wondering which breeds are best for egg production. With so many different chicken breeds to choose from, it can be overwhelming to decide which ones will give you the most eggs, fit your climate, and work for your family. Here are five chicken breeds that are known for their egg-laying abilities and are low maintenance for new chicken owners.

### Leghorn

The Leghorn is a popular choice for egg production due to its high egg output and energetic personality. These chickens are known for laying large, white eggs and can lay up to 280 eggs per year.

### Sussex

The Sussex is another excellent egg layer, producing around 250 eggs per year. These chickens are easy to care for and have a calm, docile personality. Sussex chickens come in a variety of colors and are known for their distinctive feather patterns.

### Plymouth Rock

The Plymouth Rock is a reliable egg producer, laying around 200 eggs per year. These chickens are hardy and adaptable, making them a good choice for beginner chicken owners. Plymouth Rocks are known for their friendly, docile nature and come in a variety of colors.

### Australorp

The Australorp is an Australian breed known for its high egg production, with the ability to lay up to 250 eggs per year. These chickens are hardy, adaptable, and have a calm, friendly personality.

### Rhode Island Red

The Rhode Island Red is a popular choice for egg production, with the ability to lay around 280 eggs per year. These chickens are hardy and adaptable, and they have a calm, docile personality. Rhode Island Reds come in a variety of colors and are known for their red feathers and distinctive combs.

**Note:** This list is not meant to be comprehensive in anyway. Instead, it is intended to be a quick and easy starting point for new chicken owneres! There are A LOT of chicken breeds out there and I may not have named your favorite. Feel free to add your suggestion in the comments 👇

When choosing a chicken breed for egg production, it’s important to consider your specific needs and preferences. Do you want a lot of eggs or just a few? Do you have the space for a large flock or just a few chickens? Do you want a chicken that is friendly and docile, or are you looking for a more energetic breed? Once you have a clear idea of what you’re looking for, you’ll be able to choose the best chicken breed for your needs.
